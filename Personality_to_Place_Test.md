# The Personality-to-Place Inventory

An MBTI-style personality test in 563 questions that ends with a recommendation for the U.S. city, state, ZIP code, and neighborhood that fits your personality best.

## Introduction

Most personality tests stop at a four-letter type. This one keeps going. Parts I through V measure the classic four preference dimensions (Extraversion/Introversion, Sensing/Intuition, Thinking/Feeling, Judging/Perceiving) plus an optional Assertive/Turbulent identity scale. Each dimension is broken into sub-dimensions, edge cases, and real-life scenarios. Parts VI through XV then look at your career, tastes, life stage, values, stress and conflict style, social energy, curiosity, physical-environment needs, subcultures, and dealbreakers.

The scoring section turns your answers into a four-letter type and a 20-scale Place Profile. It applies your dealbreakers as filters and matches you against a database of 73 real American neighborhoods and communities, from Brooklyn brownstones and Seattle's Capitol Hill to Amish country in Ohio, Montana ski towns, Sun Belt suburbs, and Driftless farm towns. The goal is not the place someone like you most often lives. It is the place where your personality has the best chance to thrive.

## Instructions

- Length: 563 numbered questions. Expect roughly 90 to 150 minutes. You can split it across several sittings; a natural break comes after Part V (question 216).
- Answer honestly, as you actually are on an ordinary week, not as you wish you were or think you should be. Gut answers are usually the most accurate.
- Choose exactly one letter per question. If two options fit, pick the one that fits slightly better. If none fit, pick the closest. You may skip a question that truly does not apply; skipped questions are simply not scored.
- Agreement questions use five options: A) Strongly agree, B) Agree, C) Neutral, D) Disagree, E) Strongly disagree.
- Any option labeled "Prefer not to say" is never scored and never counts against you.
- Write your answers on a sheet as number-letter pairs (for example, 1-B, 2-D, 3-A). You will need them for the scoring steps at the end.
- Civic and spiritual questions use non-partisan, non-sectarian language. No answer is better than another; they exist only to match you with communities where you would feel at home.
- Important: this inventory is for fun and self-reflection. It is not a clinical, diagnostic, or scientifically validated instrument, it is not the official Myers-Briggs Type Indicator®, and it is not affiliated with the Myers-Briggs Company. Location ratings are approximate editorial judgments. Visit, research, and talk to locals before making any real-life move.

## PART V — Identity: Assertive (A) vs. Turbulent (T)

## Scoring Methodology

Scoring has nine steps. Steps 1 and 2 give your personality type. Steps 3 to 5 build your Place Profile. Steps 6 to 9 filter and rank the location database. A pencil, your answer sheet, and a calculator are enough, and a spreadsheet makes it faster.

1. Score the four MBTI-style dimensions and find your four-letter type and clarity (Parts I–IV).
2. Score the optional Assertive/Turbulent identity (Part V).
3. Score the 20 Place-Fit Scales from Parts VI–XIV (each 0–10).
4. Tally your Career Ecosystem and pick your top two sectors.
5. Tally your Subculture Tags and keep your top five active tags.
6. Apply your Dealbreakers (Part XV) as filters on the location database.
7. Compute the Base Fit for each surviving location using the weighted-distance formula, including your priority weights.
8. Add bonuses: Career Ecosystem, Subculture Tags, and the modest MBTI Affinity layer.
9. Rank, apply tie-breakers, and write up your top match and two runners-up using the Results Template.

### Step 1 — MBTI-Style Type (Parts I–IV)

Every question in Parts I–IV awards 0, 1, or 2 points to one pole of its dimension. Use the answer key below: find your question number and the letter you chose. The cell tells you which pole gets points and how many (for example, "I2" = 2 points to Introversion; "0" = no points). Agreement questions (marked "Likert") follow the same table. Some questions list their options in reverse order on purpose, so always use the key rather than assuming A is one pole.

Each dimension has 48 questions, and each question gives at most 2 points, so the two poles of a dimension share at most 96 points in total (either pole can reach 96 on its own only if every answer went its way). Neutral Likert answers (C) give 0 points, so most people total well under 96. Part V has 24 questions, so Assertive and Turbulent share at most 48 points.

1. For each dimension, add up the points for each pole (for example, total E points and total I points).
2. Percentage for a pole = 100 × (that pole's points) ÷ (both poles' points combined). For example, E = 22 and I = 58 gives I = 72.5% and E = 27.5%. (If a dimension somehow totals 0 points, treat it as 50/50.)
3. Your letter for that dimension is the pole above 50%. If it is exactly 50/50, use the conventional tie-break: I, N, F, or P.
4. Round the winning percentage to the nearest whole number (halves round up, so 72.5% becomes 73% and 65.8% becomes 66%). Clarity uses that whole number: 50–55% = Slight, 56–65% = Moderate, 66–80% = Clear, 81–100% = Very Clear. The same rounded number is used for the 56% threshold in the MBTI Affinity bonus (Step 8).
5. Your four-letter type combines the four winning letters (for example, INFP). Report it with clarity, for example "INFP (I Clear 73%, N Clear 78%, F Clear 74%, P Moderate 63%)".

| Winning % | Clarity | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| 50–55% | Slight | Flexible; you use both poles and context decides. |
| 56–65% | Moderate | A real but adaptable preference. |
| 66–80% | Clear | A consistent, recognizable preference. |
| 81–100% | Very Clear | A defining trait; environments that ignore it will feel draining. |

#### How to score a scale

1. Find every question that feeds the scale in the Place-Fit Answer Key below. Each row shows the points each letter earns on one scale. A dash (—) means that answer is not scored for that scale, and the question then drops out of that scale's denominator.
2. Points earned = sum of the points for the letters you chose.
3. Maximum available = 10 for every multiple-choice row where your answer was scored for that scale, plus 4 for every agreement (Likert) row where it was scored. Every number in a multiple-choice row is a position on the 0–10 scale (for example, URB 5 = a mid-sized, middle-density place), so a middle answer earns middle credit even when no option in that row reaches 10.
4. Scale score = 10 × (points earned) ÷ (maximum available), rounded to one decimal. In other words, it is the average position of your answers on the 0–10 scale, with agreement answers stretched from 0–4 to 0–10. If no question for a scale was scored, set it to 5.0 (neutral).
5. Agreement items marked L+ score A=4, B=3, C=2, D=1, E=0; items marked L− score A=0, B=1, C=2, D=3, E=4. These are already written out in the key.
6. You can match with the one-decimal scores directly. If you prefer whole numbers (as the worked examples do), round to the nearest whole number with halves rounding up. A score is "strong" for weighting in Step 7 if it is 2 or less, or 8 or more.

Example: you answer three COST questions: two multiple-choice items where your answers are worth 10 and 6, and one agreement item where you earn 3 of 4. Points earned = 19 and maximum available = 10 + 10 + 4 = 24, so COST = 10 × 19 ÷ 24 = 7.9, meaning you are quite cost-sensitive.

### Step 7 — Matching Formula (Base Fit)

For every surviving location, compute a distance d on each of the 20 scales between your score U and the location rating Lc:

- Symmetric scales (URB, PACE, HEAT, SEAS, DRY, ARTS, OUT, NIGHT, SOC, CIV, FAITH, FAM, WALK, DIV): d = |U − Lc|.
- Cost: d = max(0, U_COST − Lc_AFF). You are penalized only when a place is less affordable than your sensitivity allows. A cheaper place costs nothing.
- Nature and quiet scales (MTN, WATER, DESERT, FOREST, QUIET): d = max(0, U − Lc) + 0.5 × max(0, Lc − U). Missing something you want gets the full penalty. Having something you do not care about gets half.

Weights. Each scale starts at weight w = 1.0. Multiply by 1.5 if your score is strong (2 or less, or 8 or more), because strong preferences matter more. Then multiply by 2.0 if the scale is in your Priority #1 group, and by 1.5 if it is in your Priority #2 group.

Base Fit = 100 × [ 1 − ( Σ w × d ) ÷ ( 10 × Σ w ) ]. A Base Fit of 100 is a perfect match on every scale, and each point of weighted average distance costs 10 points.

### Step 9 — Final Fit Score, Ranking, and Tie-Breakers

Final Fit Score = Base Fit + Career bonus + Subculture bonus + MBTI Affinity bonus. The score can go above 100. Rank all surviving locations by Final Fit, rounded to one decimal. The highest is your Top Match, and the next two are your Runners-Up. If two locations tie, break the tie in this order:

1. Higher Base Fit (personality-environment fit beats bonuses).
2. Fewer strong-preference violations: count the scales with weight ≥ 1.5 where d ≥ 4. Fewer is better.
3. Smaller Civic Climate gap |U_CIV − Lc_CIV|.
4. Smaller cost gap (d on COST).
5. If still tied, the location in a region you have never lived in wins, because the test favors discovery.

Sanity check: if your Top Match has a d of 6 or more on any Priority #1 scale, read the runner-ups carefully. A large single gap can hide behind a high average.
